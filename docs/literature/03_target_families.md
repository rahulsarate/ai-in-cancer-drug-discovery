# Target Families for First Prioritization

## AXL / TAM receptors

Genes: `AXL`, `MERTK`, `TYRO3`, `GAS6`, `PROS1`

Rationale: AXL is strongly linked to EMT, invasion, therapy resistance, immune evasion, and TAM/CAF ligand support. It is tractable but context-dependent.

Pros:

- Drug-like target family.
- Strong EMT/metastasis connection.
- Good combination-therapy logic with immunotherapy or targeted therapy.

Cons:

- TAM receptor redundancy.
- On-target immune and tissue effects need safety review.

## TGF-beta / SMAD

Genes: `TGFB1`, `TGFB2`, `TGFB3`, `TGFBR1`, `TGFBR2`, `SMAD2`, `SMAD3`, `SMAD4`

Rationale: Core EMT inducer and TME remodeler.

Pros:

- Central biology.
- Strong link to CAFs, immune exclusion, and late-stage metastasis.

Cons:

- Double-edged biology: suppressive in early tumorigenesis, pro-metastatic later.
- Patient selection is essential.

## YAP/TAZ/TEAD

Genes: `YAP1`, `WWTR1`, `TEAD1-4`, `LATS1`, `LATS2`

Rationale: Integrates mechanical stress, ECM stiffness, invasion, survival, and tumor regeneration.

Pros:

- Excellent spatial-niche biology.
- TEAD inhibitors create translational opportunity.

Cons:

- Pathway is broad and tissue-regenerative.
- Biomarker selection is still hard.

## FAK/SRC/integrins

Genes: `PTK2`, `SRC`, `ITGA5`, `ITGB1`, `ITGAV`, `ITGB3`

Rationale: Invasion, adhesion, ECM remodeling, anoikis resistance, and CAF-rich niches.

Pros:

- Drug-like signaling nodes.
- Strong spatial and invasion relevance.

Cons:

- Previous trials have had mixed results.
- May require combinations.

## Ferroptosis axis

Genes: `GPX4`, `SLC7A11`, `ACSL4`, `LPCAT3`, `SCD`, `FADS2`, `AIFM2`, `TFRC`

Rationale: Mesenchymal, therapy-resistant, and ZEB1-high states may expose lipid-peroxidation vulnerabilities.

Pros:

- Creative and mechanistically distinct.
- Good link to state-specific vulnerability rather than generic killing.

Cons:

- Direct GPX4 targeting has safety and druggability challenges.
- Ferroptosis assays vary and require careful validation.

## CAF/TAM crosstalk

Genes: `CSF1R`, `CCR2`, `CXCR4`, `CXCL12`, `SPP1`, `TREM2`, `IL6`, `JAK1`, `JAK2`

Rationale: EMT cells are often supported by macrophage/fibroblast niches.

Pros:

- Strong TME and immunotherapy relevance.
- Good for spatial transcriptomics and ligand-receptor modeling.

Cons:

- Ligand-receptor inference can overpredict.
- Immune modulation requires careful safety framing.

## Hypoxia and metabolism

Genes: `HIF1A`, `EPAS1`, `CA9`, `VEGFA`, `SLC2A1`, `LDHA`, `PDK1`

Rationale: Hypoxic niches promote EMT, invasion, immune suppression, and metabolic adaptation.

Pros:

- Strong tumor ecology signal.
- Spatially interpretable.

Cons:

- HIF biology is broad.
- Hypoxia signatures may reflect poor perfusion rather than targetable dependency.

## PRC2/KMT2D and EMT chromatin regulators

Genes: `EZH2`, `EED`, `SUZ12`, `KMT2D`, `KDM6A`, `ARID1A`

Rationale: Chromatin changes can stabilize EMT, plasticity, and therapy-resistant states.

Pros:

- Links plasticity to durable epigenetic states.
- Several druggable epigenetic nodes.

Cons:

- Context is highly cancer-type specific.
- Epigenetic inhibitors may have broad effects.

## RNA-binding and P-body regulators

Genes: `DDX6`, `EDC4`, `LSM14A`, `DCP1A`, `DCP2`, `TNRC6A`, `AGO2`

Rationale: Recent CRISPR-style studies suggest RNA-processing/P-body biology can regulate EMT/migration.

Pros:

- Creative, less crowded target space.
- Good discovery angle.

Cons:

- Druggability and safety are uncertain.
- Requires strong validation before translation.
